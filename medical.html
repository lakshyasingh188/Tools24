What Is a Medical Report Simplifier?

A medical report simplifier is an online tool that reads your medical report and organises the test names, values, units and reference ranges into clearer, simpler language. Medical reports often contain abbreviations, numbers and technical terms that can be difficult to understand. AGITools24 Medical Report Simplifier helps you see what is written in your report without changing any medical values. It is an educational tool, not a diagnostic service, and it does not replace the advice of a qualified healthcare professional.

How Does the AGITools24 Medical Report Simplifier Work?

The AGITools24 Medical Report Simplifier follows a simple five-step process:

  • Upload — You upload a PDF or image of your medical report.
  • Read — The tool attempts to read the text from your file using browser-based text extraction or OCR.
  • Organise — It identifies test names, values, units and reference ranges printed on the report.
  • Explain — It presents the information in simple English, Hindi or Hinglish with educational explanations.
  • Download — You can download a PDF, print the summary, or copy the text.

The tool always prefers the reference range printed on your report over any generic range. It does not invent values or reference ranges.

How to Read a Blood Test Report

A blood test report usually contains the following columns:

  • Test Name — The name of the test, such as Hemoglobin or Glucose.
  • Result — The numerical value measured in your sample.
  • Unit — The unit of measurement, such as g/dL, mg/dL or U/L.
  • Reference Range — The range provided by the laboratory for comparison.
  • Status — Whether the result is within, above or below the reference range.

AGITools24 helps you read these columns by organising them into a clean, simple format.

How to Understand CBC Report

A Complete Blood Count (CBC) is one of the most common blood tests. It may include:

  • Hemoglobin (Hb) — A protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.
  • RBC (Red Blood Cell Count) — The number of red blood cells in your blood.
  • WBC (White Blood Cell Count) — Cells that help fight infection.
  • Platelets — Cell fragments that help with blood clotting.
  • Hematocrit (HCT/PCV) — The proportion of blood made up of red blood cells.
  • MCV, MCH, MCHC — Measurements of red blood cell size and hemoglobin content.

How to Understand LFT Report

A Liver Function Test (LFT) may include:

  • SGPT (ALT) — An enzyme found mainly in the liver.
  • SGOT (AST) — An enzyme found in the liver, heart and muscles.
  • ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase) — An enzyme related to bile ducts and bones.
  • Bilirubin — A byproduct of red blood cell breakdown.
  • Total Protein, Albumin, Globulin — Proteins produced by the liver.

How to Understand KFT Report

A Kidney Function Test (KFT) may include:

  • Urea — A waste product filtered by the kidneys.
  • Creatinine — A waste product from muscle activity, filtered by kidneys.
  • Uric Acid — A waste product from the breakdown of purines.

How to Understand Lipid Profile

A lipid profile measures fats in your blood:

  • Total Cholesterol — The total amount of cholesterol in your blood.
  • HDL — Often called "good" cholesterol.
  • LDL — Often called "bad" cholesterol.
  • Triglycerides — A type of fat stored in the body.
  • VLDL — Very low-density lipoprotein.

How to Understand Thyroid Report

A thyroid panel may include:

  • TSH — Thyroid Stimulating Hormone, produced by the pituitary gland.
  • T3 — Triiodothyronine, an active thyroid hormone.
  • T4 — Thyroxine, the main hormone produced by the thyroid gland.

These values help understand how the thyroid gland is functioning. Only a doctor can interpret them in your clinical context.

How to Understand Blood Sugar Report

Blood sugar tests may include:

  • Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS) — Measured after not eating for 8–12 hours.
  • Post-Prandial Blood Sugar (PPBS) — Measured 2 hours after a meal.
  • Random Blood Sugar (RBS) — Measured at any time of day.
  • HbA1c — Reflects average blood sugar over the past 2–3 months.

Why Reference Ranges Matter

Reference ranges are the values that a laboratory considers "normal" for a healthy population. However, reference ranges can differ between laboratories, testing methods, age groups, gender and other factors. A value outside the reference range does not always mean there is a problem. Only a qualified healthcare professional can interpret your results in the context of your symptoms, medical history and other test results. AGITools24 always prefers the reference range printed on your uploaded report.

Can a Medical Report Simplifier Replace a Doctor?

No. A medical report simplifier cannot replace a doctor. It only organises and explains the information already present in your report in simpler language. It does not diagnose diseases, prescribe medication or recommend treatment. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional for medical advice, diagnosis and treatment. Tools24 and AGITools24 medical tools are designed to support, not replace, professional medical care.
